Ted Nelson's 1967 Vision: Hypertext That Breathes With the Reader

In 1967, hypertext pioneer Ted Nelson coined the term "stretchtext" to describe a form of non-sequential writing where the reader controls the throttle of detail continuously in place. Instead of jumping away via footnotes or losing context in separate tabs, a reader starts with a crisp one-sixth distillation of the page and pulls open individual sentences right where curiosity strikes, shrinking them back when satisfied.

For nearly six decades, stretchtext remained rare on the open web because human authors had to manually write three or four telescoping versions of every single paragraph. With Pollinations language models and Bring Your Own Pollen (BYOP), the Polli StretchText browser extension compiles multi-depth telescoping layers on the fly for any article, documentation page, or essay you visit.

When you click "Condense Article to 1/6× StretchText" above (or in the browser extension popup), each long paragraph collapses into its core thesis chip. Clicking "+ Stretch more" expands that specific paragraph to its half-length supporting context, and clicking "+ Full text" reveals the author's verbatim original prose without ever scrolling away from your reading position.

Install Browser Extension (MV3)

Works in Chrome, Edge, Brave, and Chromium browsers via Manifest V3, with OAuth 2.0 Device Flow (`https://enter.pollinations.ai/api/device/code`) so readers pay with their own Pollen.

  1. Clone or download Brutus88Ai/polli-stretchtext.
  2. Open chrome://extensions and enable Developer mode.
  3. Click Load unpacked and select the repository folder.
  4. Pin Polli StretchText, connect your Pollinations wallet via Device Flow (or use 0-Pollen Local Telescoping), and condense any web page in one click!
Zero-Pollen Guarantee: Repeatedly stretching and shrinking paragraphs uses local level caching and deterministic fallback telescoping so exploring costs 0 extra Pollen.
